Onboarding for Buyer

Redesigning FashionGo's buyer onboarding journey — from the first sign-in prompt through a four-step account, business, and preference wizard — for both desktop and mobile

Role:Product Designer Scope:Desktop + Mobile Company:FashionGo (NHN Global)
Buyer Onboarding Multi-Step Wizard Google SSO Responsive Design

The Challenge

Becoming a verified buyer on FashionGo isn't a single sign-up form — it's a sequence of trust-building steps: proving you're a real person, proving you're a real business, and telling FashionGo enough about your store to get matched with the right vendors. Each step is a place a buyer can stall or drop off, and each one needed to work cleanly on both desktop and mobile, where screen space and attention are very different. I designed the full flow end to end — the entry modal, the four-step wizard (Welcome, Basics, Business, For You), real-time password and email verification, and a mobile-specific adaptation of each step — balancing how much friction is actually necessary to verify a legitimate wholesale buyer against how much is just friction.

Figma Design Systems Responsive / Mobile Adaptation

Key Design Decisions

01

A Low-Friction Entry Point

A single "Sign In or Create Account" modal with one email field and a Continue with Google shortcut replaces making visitors decide up front whether they're signing in or creating an account — on both desktop and mobile.

02

A Four-Step Wizard

A persistent tab bar (Welcome → Basics → Business → For You) keeps buyers oriented. Step one branches immediately into "I am a buyer" or "I am a vendor," since the two groups need almost entirely different information from there.

03

Verification Without Derailing the Flow

Password requirements show as a live checklist instead of a post-submit error, and email is verified in place with a 5-digit code — on mobile, as a bottom-sheet overlay directly on top of the form — rather than an email link that bounces the buyer out of the flow.

04

Business Verification, Trimmed for Mobile

Desktop collects the full business picture — company, type, address, seller's permit — in one pass. Mobile cuts it down to the fields that matter most for verification and defers the rest, instead of asking someone to fill out a long form on a small screen.

05

Personalizing From Day One

The last step opens into a tag-based preference picker — product categories, style, business attributes like Made in USA or Private Label, and identity preferences like Women-Owned or Eco Friendly — so FashionGo can start surfacing relevant vendors from a buyer's very first session.
